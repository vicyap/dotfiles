# Foundations: People, Cognition, And Behavior

Start with context. Identify the audience, their vocabulary and skill, the goal
behind the requested operation, and the conditions in which they act. Direct
observation and concrete scenarios reveal workflow better than demographic
assumptions. Research for design should explain behavior and unmet needs, not
merely market preference.

## Behavioral Patterns

### Safe Exploration
Let people investigate without irreversible consequences. Provide clear exits,
reversible actions, previews, and preserved state; avoid dead ends.

### Instant Gratification
Offer a useful result or visible progress early. Defer setup and advanced choices
until their value is apparent, without hiding requirements that affect consent or
cost.

### Satisficing
People often choose the first credible option rather than compare everything.
Put common, safe choices first and make labels scannable; do not depend on users
discovering the theoretically optimal path.

### Changes In Midstream
Expect goals, inputs, and context to change during a task. Preserve work, allow
backtracking, and avoid forcing a complete restart when scope changes.

### Deferred Choices
Postpone nonessential decisions until the user has enough context. Supply a safe
default and make later revision discoverable.

### Incremental Construction
Support building a result in small visible steps. Keep intermediate work usable,
saveable, and correctable instead of requiring a perfect specification upfront.

### Habituation
Repeated actions become automatic. Keep routine placement and behavior stable;
separate destructive commands from habitual targets and require confirmation
only where consequence warrants interruption.

### Microbreaks
Use unavoidable short waits for orientation or low-effort value, but never add a
delay to create one. Keep progress honest and preserve attention.

### Spatial Memory
Stable locations help recall. Avoid arbitrary reordering and layout shifts;
preserve position when returning to a workspace or collection.

### Prospective Memory
People rely on the interface to remember future intentions. Surface reminders,
pending states, drafts, and next actions at the relevant time and place.

### Streamlined Repetition
Make repeated work faster through retained context, repeat commands, templates,
batching, or sensible prefill. Keep each repetition inspectable and reversible.

### Keyboard Only
Support complete, ordered, visible keyboard operation for frequent work and
accessibility. Do not reserve essential actions for pointer gestures.

### Social Proof And Collaboration
Show credible activity, ownership, presence, or shared outcomes when it helps
people judge or coordinate. Protect privacy and do not manufacture urgency or
popularity.

## Review Checklist

- Does the interface use the user's language and expose their goal?
- Can people explore, revise, interrupt, and resume without losing work?
- Are defaults safe, useful, and changeable?
- Do repeated and expert workflows become faster without blocking novices?
- Does the interface carry memory that would otherwise burden the user?
