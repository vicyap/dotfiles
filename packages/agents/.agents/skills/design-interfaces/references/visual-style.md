# Visual Style, Aesthetics, And Accessibility

Use composition, color, typography, imagery, and rhythm to clarify structure and
evoke an appropriate feeling. Style is functional: it changes what people notice,
read, trust, and understand. Preserve readability under zoom, text expansion,
high contrast, reduced motion, and assistive technology.

## Foundations

- Use contrast and scale to express hierarchy, not decoration alone.
- Choose colors by role and verify contrast; never rely on color as the only cue.
- Use a restrained type system with readable line length, spacing, and hierarchy.
- Align paragraphs for scanning; avoid centered body copy and overlong measures.
- Use imagery to convey content, identity, or instruction, with meaningful
  alternatives where the image carries information.
- Keep enterprise and dense interfaces calm and legible rather than visually
  flattening all importance.

## Style Ranges

### Skeuomorphic
Use familiar physical cues when they teach operation or create appropriate
character. Remove imitation that reduces density, clarity, or accessibility.

### Illustrated
Use illustration to explain, orient, or establish a distinctive tone. Keep it
subordinate to tasks and provide alternatives for instructional content.

### Flat
Use clean surfaces and restrained ornament while retaining strong affordances,
boundaries, focus, and interactive states. Flat must not mean ambiguous.

### Minimalistic
Remove elements that do not support the goal, but retain labels, context, and
recovery. Simplicity is reduced cognitive work, not merely fewer visible pixels.

### Adaptive Or Parametric
Let appearance respond to data, context, environment, or user settings when the
change communicates useful state. Keep meaning predictable and user control
available where adaptation can surprise.

## Accessibility Checklist

- Heading order, landmarks, labels, names, roles, and values are programmatic.
- Focus order follows the task and focus remains visible and restored after
  overlays or navigation.
- Text and controls survive zoom, reflow, localization, and content expansion.
- Target size and spacing tolerate motor imprecision.
- Status, error, selection, and priority use more than color.
- Motion, transparency, contrast, and theme respect platform preferences.
- Images and charts expose equivalent meaning, not redundant descriptions.

When detailed visual implementation is requested, combine this reasoning with
the project's design system and an applicable visual-design skill.
